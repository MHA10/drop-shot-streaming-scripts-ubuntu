Who's who

Each card is one person numberThe computer gave a number to everyone it could tell apart by what they wear. One player can have several numbers. That's expected.
Same person on two cards?Tick both cards, then press Same person. Their stats add up straight away.
Dashed boxes are suggestionsCards the computer thinks are probably the same player are shown together. Check the pictures: if they're all one person press Same person: all; if not, tick just the ones that are, or press Not the same / done.
Most players have 2+ cardsUsually one for each half of the court: from the far half the camera sees a small player from the front, from the near half a bigger one from the back, and the computer plays safe. A player who sits out 5+ minutes also gets a new card. When the teams change ends, the computer spots a player's two cards taking turns on court and boxes them together.
Plays the left / right sideThe side of their own half a player stands on, as padel players name it (seen from behind them). Partners split left and right and keep their side all match, so the computer never boxes a left-side card with a right-side one.
Two people on one card?Press Two people in here. That is the mistake that matters most, so please flag every one you see. The computer marks cards that stayed on one half while the players changed ends, with pictures from just before and after: type flagged in find to see them.
Not a player?A referee, a ball collector or someone waiting: press Not a player.
TagsEach card is described in words (shirt, shorts, cap, a name or number on the shirt). Type in find to filter, for example "red" or "SALEH". Fix or add tags with Edit tags; kit numbers usually need adding by hand for now.

Who was on court when

One row per number, with time along the bottom. Click a row to jump to its card.

Rows show numbers on court for at least the "short" limit. Merged numbers show on one row.
