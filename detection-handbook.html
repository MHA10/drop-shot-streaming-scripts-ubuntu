01

The pipeline, end to end

Two independent inputs — the match audio and the match video — are read separately, then fused. The audio says when a shot happened; the video says where everyone was. Almost everything downstream is built from those two facts.

Audio lane — listening
1
Find the cracksEvery carbon-paddle strike is a sharp broadband crack. Mark each one across the whole match.
2
Group into ralliesA burst of cracks is a point; the quiet between bursts is the gap. No scoreboard is read.
3
Attribute the endThe mic is nearer one end, so that end sounds louder — which tells us which side hit.
Video lane — watching
1
Calibrate the courtColour-mask the blue court → a homography that turns any pixel into a court position.
2
Track the four playersYOLO finds the people; each is given a fixed A/B/C/D letter and followed frame to frame.
3
Read the ball (when visible)TrackNet reads 3 stacked frames and guesses the ball's position — sparse on our camera.
Fused — the two lanes combined
Shot count Which team Which player Attack vs defence Serve & server Player heatmaps Per-player stats Rally boundaries Over-count cleanup

What we detect, and how each one is identified

The core of this handbook. Each card is one thing the system works out, the method behind it, and the tell-tale cue it keys on. Filter by how ready it is, or by which signal it comes from.

Status
Signal
Shots & scoring

Shot timing

Working

When was each ball actually struck?

Method
Listens to the whole match and marks every paddle strike — no video, no scoreboard.
Tell-tale cue
A sudden energy spike (an onset) in the ~2.5–9 kHz band, rising above a rolling baseline. Nothing else on a padel court sounds like a carbon paddle.
Audiomatch_points.py

Rally boundaries

Working

Where does each point start and end?

Method
Groups cracks into bursts, then asks the video to settle the two cases audio gets wrong: a long lob (3 s of silence mid-rally) and a serve fault (a lone burst).
Tell-tale cue
Gap length between strikes, checked against whether players stood in a serve formation at that moment.
Fusedmatch_points.py · match_refine.py

Which end hit

Working

Which side of the net played the shot?

Method
The mic sits nearer one end, so an identical shot is louder from that end. Loudness places each strike on an end.
Tell-tale cue
Relative loudness between ends, tightened by the rule that a padel ball must alternate ends every touch.
Audiomatch_contacts.py

Which team

Working

Which of the two teams hit it?

Method
In padel each pair stays on its own half for the whole game, so the end already is the team — near = Team A, far = Team B.
Tell-tale cue
Court end → fixed team mapping. No extra detection needed.
Fusedmatch_shots.py

Which player

Working

Which of the four hit the ball?

Method
At the instant of the crack the striker is swinging and the other three are not, so their outline changes far more between frames. When the ball is detected, we correct to the player it's actually on.
Tell-tale cue
Biggest frame-to-frame body motion in the ~100 ms around the strike (a "swing score"); confidence = the margin over the 2nd-biggest mover.
Fusedmatch_lastshot.py

Attack vs defence

Working

Net shot, or a scramble off the back glass?

Method
Reads the hitter's depth on the court at the moment of contact and bands it at 3 m — the service line.
Tell-tale cue
Within 3 m of the net → attacking; within 3 m of the back glass → defending.
Videomatch_shots.py

Serve & server

Working

Which end served each point, from where?

Method
At a point's start the four players form a giveaway shape; that shape plus the serve-rotation rule pins the serving end.
Tell-tale cue
Server deep in a back corner, partner at the net, opponents back — and the rule that the serving end holds two games, then flips.
Videomatch_serves.py
Players & tracking

Player tracking (A/B/C/D)

Working

Where is each of the four, every frame?

Method
YOLO detects the people; a letter is handed out once by starting corner (top-left = A …) and then belongs to the person, even across the court.
Tell-tale cue
Person boxes projected through the court homography to a top-down position.
Videotrack_players.py · YOLO11s

Player identity

Working

Which real person is which, across a whole match?

Method
Recognises each by kit — shirt colour and shorts colour — so identity survives the change of ends.
Tell-tale cue
A kit-colour signature (a histogram over a shared colour codebook). Flagged automatically when two kits are too alike to separate.
Videomatch_players.py

Heatmaps

Working

Where did each player spend the point?

Method
A long-exposure of each player's feet: accumulate their position over the clip and stain a top-down court.
Tell-tale cue
Dwell time per court cell — green where they passed through, red where they camped.
Videoplayer_heatmap.py

Per-player stats

Working

Distance, speed, coverage, net-vs-back time.

Method
Falls straight out of the A/B/C/D tracks once you know where everyone was — no ball needed anywhere.
Tell-tale cue
Track geometry over time: metres run, top speed, area covered, time at the net vs the glass, and how well a pair holds its shape.
Videoplayer_stats.py

Session split

Working

Is this one match, or a whole evening?

Method
Splits a long recording wherever the set of people on court changes and stays changed — so a league night isn't read as one impossible match.
Tell-tale cue
A shift in the kit signature across points.
Videomatch_sessions.py
Ball & court

Ball position

Partial

Where is the ball right now?

Method
A neural tracker (TrackNet) reads 3 stacked frames → a heat-map of the likely ball → fits a circle. Filtered to inside the court and gap-filled between hits.
Tell-tale cue
The 3-frame motion signature of a small, fast object. On tennis weights it catches the ball only where it's clearly moving — sparse and noisy on our camera.
VideoTrackNet · tracknet.pt

Court calibration

Working

Where is the court inside the frame?

Method
Colour-mask the blue playing surface, fit a homography, and turn any pixel into normalised court coordinates. Everything geometric leans on this.
Tell-tale cue
The court's blue colour and known rectangle → coordinates where the net sits at the halfway line.
Videocalibrate() · match_lastshot.py
Cleanup & fusion — added this cycle

Echo / wall over-count filter

Working

Was that "shot" real, or an echo / wall hit?

Method
Drops a counted shot only when all three hold at once — the fingerprint of a non-paddle sound.
Tell-tale cue
Unsure who hit (low margin) + no ball nearby + crammed against another shot (<0.7 s). Took one rally from 15 → 11 real shots.
Fusedrally cleanup

Post-point noise filter

Working

Did the point already end?

Method
Once the ball is seen leaving play and not coming back, every later sound (a clap, the pick-up) is ignored.
Tell-tale cue
Ball detected past a baseline with no return for the rest of the rally.
Fusedrally cleanup

Teammate ball-fix

Working

Right team, but was it the right teammate?

Method
The motion-based player pick can grab the wrong same-side teammate. When the ball is visible, the box snaps to the player it's actually on.
Tell-tale cue
Ball-to-player distance among the players on the hitting side.
Fusedrally cleanup

No detectors match this filter.

The models & methods behind it

Four building blocks carry the whole system, and a few approaches were tried and set aside. Being clear about the second list matters as much as the first — it's what we already know not to spend time on.

dead ends, documented
YOLO for the ball

Locked onto a static court-side logo (zero motion) instead of the ball. A general object detector can't isolate a tiny, fast, low-contrast ball.

Rejected

None found

Using the noisy tennis ball to cancel shots wrongly killed real ones. Reworked into additive cues (attribute / clean up), never a veto.

Reworked

Trained well (see next section) but doesn't transfer to our camera. Kept as a starting point, not shipped.

04

The ball fine-tune — what happened

We re-trained the tennis ball-finder on 100k labelled padel frames (pro-tour footage) on a free cloud GPU. It worked — and then taught us something important about why it still isn't enough.

0.007
First score — the model found a lazy shortcut and predicted "no ball" everywhere (most frames have no ball).
0.78
Best score after removing the shortcut (train only on frames that have a ball). Genuinely good on pro footage.
~80%
Of visible balls found, on the pro-tour validation video it practised on.
1/3
As many detections on our camera vs the plain tennis model — it got worse, not better, here.

Detections on our own footage

rally 18 · same clip · same pipeline
Tennis model204
Padel model (pro-trained)63

The lesson: a padel dataset isn't enough — the ball-finder has to practise on our camera. The pro model learned padel balls in a bright, close, TV-broadcast look; our fixed, dim, security-style angle is a different visual world, so it transfers poorly. Input-geometry tweaks were tested and ruled out — it's the domain, not a setting.

05

What's pending

The audio-driven side (shots, teams, players, stats, heatmaps) is solid today. The open items all trace back to one thing: a ball-finder that works on our own camera.

The real fix — do first

Fine-tune on our own footage

Label a few hundred frames from the Padel Central camera and continue training from the pro model. This unlocks the two items below. Bottleneck is human marking time, not video count or compute.

Gated on the ball

Metal-net & wall veto

Flag the "shot" that was really a ball into the fence. Needs a ball reliably detected at the contact instant — which today's tracker misses exactly there.

Gated on the ball

Rally-tail robustness

The end-of-point cases (ball dead / picked up) rely on a ball track that currently flip-flops at the tail. A reliable ball makes the post-point filter trustworthy.

Validation

Match-wide threshold check

The cleanup filters (echo, post-point) are tuned on one rally. Run them across the full match and spot-check before trusting the numbers everywhere.

One-line status: the listening-based detection — counting shots, teams, players, stats, heatmaps — works and is now much cleaner. Everything still open comes down to teaching the ball-finder to see our specific camera, and we've mapped exactly how to do it.
